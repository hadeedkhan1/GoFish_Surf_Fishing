# GoFish: Surf Fishing (Hackathon Winner - Beach Hack Track)

**Beginner-friendly surf and pier fishing trip planner for Galveston, Texas.**

**[Devpost Submission](https://devpost.com/software/go-fish-4okrp9) · [Quick Demo (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A9epMlD23hg)**

## Inspiration

Most fishing apps are designed for people who already fish. They assume familiarity with tide movement, bait selection, and licensing rules, creating a barrier for first-time anglers.

Galveston is a popular and accessible fishing destination on the Texas Gulf Coast, but beginners have no single place to find out whether conditions are safe, where to fish, when to go, and what they are likely to catch.

**GoFish combines all of these answers into one trip plan.**

## What It Does

GoFish generates a trip plan for beginner shore and pier fishing in Galveston, Texas. No boat is required.

- **Safety Verdict:** A Go / Caution / No rating based on live weather alerts, wind, waves, heat, and rip current statements. Each factor includes its source and last update time.
- **License Check:** Three questions determine whether a fishing license is required. For example, no license is needed to fish from shore in a Texas state park.
- **Trip Plan:** A map of three beginner-friendly locations, recommended fishing windows, and a tide chart. A "Why This Time?" panel breaks down each score, with every factor labeled as data or rule of thumb.
- **Fish Cards:** Each species has a card covering bait, rigging, and hazards. Users see a daily pick (the species most likely to bite) alongside other likely catches.
- **Collection:** All species are displayed in a deck. Uncaught species appear as silhouettes, and logging a catch unlocks the corresponding card. Catch-and-release catches count too.

## How We Built It

- **Frontend:** React, Vite, Tailwind CSS, React Router, Leaflet (maps), and Recharts (tide charts). The interface is mobile-first for outdoor use.
- **Backend:** Node.js and Express, with a central `/api/plan` endpoint that retrieves live data and assembles the fishing plan.
- **Live Data:**
  - [NOAA CO-OPS](https://tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/) — Tide predictions and water temperature from Galveston Pier 21.
  - [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/) — Weather, wind, pressure, and waves.
  - [National Weather Service](https://www.weather.gov/) — Weather alerts.
- **Curated Data:** Species information, fishing locations, license rules, and consumption advisories are maintained as hand-verified JSON, with source links to TPWD and Texas DSHS. Regulatory information is never generated by an AI model.
- **Scoring:** A custom, explainable formula weighted by tide movement, water temperature, wind, and time of day. Every fishing window's score can be traced back to its inputs.
- **Team Workflow:** A team of four using different AI tools, coordinated through a shared project brief defining the API contract, identifiers, and design decisions.

## Challenges We Ran Into

- **Rip Current Data:** Our initial rule treated missing data as caution, but the Houston/Galveston weather office does not publish a surf zone forecast, causing every day to return "Caution." We switched to NWS rip current statements while maintaining the principle that missing data is never treated as safe.
- **Daylight Saving Time:** The time change on Sunday, November 1, makes that day 25 hours long. A hardcoded UTC offset would have shifted every fishing window by an hour.
- **Scope:** We initially planned user accounts, a community feed, and AI fish identification. We cut all three to deliver one complete, polished experience.
- **Integration:** Coordinating four branches under time pressure led to a broken build after a late merge. We now run the build before every merge.

## Accomplishments We're Proud Of

- The app runs entirely on live data.
- Missing data is never presented as safe, and every value includes a source and timestamp.
- More than 100 backend tests, which caught real defects, including fishing windows extending past sunset and a stalled external API blocking the entire app.
- Users can go from entering a trip date to a complete fishing plan in just a few taps.
- A clean, readable interface designed for beginners rather than experienced anglers.

## What We Learned

- Defining the data contract first allows frontend and backend development to proceed in parallel.
- Beginner-friendly design depends on removing jargon and limiting each screen to a single decision.
- Public data has gaps, and handling those gaps transparently matters as much as the data itself.
- Teams using different AI tools need a single shared source of truth to stay consistent.

## What's Next for GoFish

- **Gamification:** Species rarity, daily quests, streaks, collection badges, and a map of currently active locations.
- **Species Information:** Photos and identification guidance on every fish card.
- **Mobile:** A progressive web app with offline support, followed by a native app.
- **Optional Accounts:** Supabase integration for cross-device collection syncing and alerts when conditions are favorable. The planner will remain free without an account.
- **Expanded Coverage:** Additional Texas coastal locations, followed by lakes and rivers.
